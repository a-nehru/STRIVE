The cast: shape characters, drawn in code

Simple shapes with a face. Bringing one to life is a bounce, a blink, pupils that follow, and a little squash when it lands, never a body to rig. Drawn live in code (no image files), so they recolour, scale and react for free, and every one is ours to publish. Move your pointer over them, click one, or pick a mood.

Mood:

What moves

Hop and land with a squash, a slow breath when idle, a blink every few seconds, pupils that look at the ball, the hand or the next target. Under calm motion: no hops, still blinks.

What changes with mood

Only the eyes, the brows and the mouth: six faces from a handful of curves. The shape and colour stay, so the character is always recognisable.

Colour scheme

The coach is yellow on every screen; each game has a character in its own accent, on warm white (#F6F2EA) with ink (#1E2235) faces and Nunito 800-900 for words.

Screens

Every screen shares the same chrome (Help top left, Today and the close button top right), the clock in the top centre with the game's one number beside it, and the coach in the bottom corner away from the trained arm. Games keep their own world; the frame around it is the same. Click a screen to enlarge it.

One flow for every game

The same five beats in the same places, so learning one game teaches where to look in all of them.

Intro cardName, one sentence, spoken. 4 s or Enter.

3, 2, 1The same countdown everywhere, soft ticks.

End moment"Time!" or "Finish!" 1.2 s. Confetti only for a milestone.

ResultsThe shared results screen, then Play again.

The coach: three options to test

Five states cover every event: waiting, a good hit or streak, a best or level up, a posture cue (the attention colour, dashed, never red), and a break. The coach is the same in every game.

Grasp prompt in the assessment

Today the open and closed hands are emoji drawn as text. They depend on the computer's emoji font (some Windows and browser setups show boxes), they are small, the two look alike from across the room, and they ignore the screen's colours. Proposed: a big drawn hand in the assessment's violet, the word under it, one step at a time, and a "We see" chip that shows what the camera sees.

Do and don't

Do

  • ✓One accent per game, and only that one.
  • ✓Flat fills, soft shadows, generous empty space.
  • ✓Big type: 32 px at the smallest for anything a patient reads.
  • ✓Shape and words with every colour (a check, a dashed ring, a word).
  • ✓Compare patients only with themselves; progress only grows.
  • ✓Celebrate milestones, once, and then settle.

Don't

  • ✕Red for a miss, buzzers, shakes or flashes.
  • ✕Gradients and glows on buttons and panels.
  • ✕Day streaks that break, lives, or lost stars.
  • ✕Numbers everywhere: one clock and one number per game.
  • ✕Baby faces and toy colours: friendly, never childish.
  • ✕Emoji as interface: they change with the computer.
